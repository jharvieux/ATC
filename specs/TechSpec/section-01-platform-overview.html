AI Travel Concierge Platform — Tech Spec v6.2

Section 1 — Platform Overview

The platform delivers AI-powered cruise travel agency services across three business models, sharing a common technical foundation. This section defines the business model, the personas the platform serves, and the high-level technical posture.

1.1 Business Model — Three Prongs ¶

Prong 1: Fully Automated Own Agency

The platform operator runs their own cruise travel agency. AI personas handle customer inquiries, recommend trips, generate quotes, and submit bookings. Commissions accrue to the platform directly. This is the platform’s first-party revenue stream.

Prong 2: BYO-Host SaaS for Independent Agents

Independent travel agents who already have their own host agency relationship (e.g., with Travel Leaders, Avoya, Cruise Planners) subscribe to the platform as software. They bring their own host agency credentials. The platform charges a per-seat subscription; commissions flow to their existing host agency arrangements without platform intermediation.

Prong 3: White-Label Sub-Host Model

Independent travel agents who don’t have their own host agency relationship subscribe to the platform AND book under the platform’s host agency. The platform takes a commission split (with tiered rates) and remits the remainder via Stripe Connect Express.

1.4 Tenant Resolution ¶

Every request to the platform resolves to a tenant context based on the request’s host:

  • Platform primary domain (e.g., ai-travelconcierge.com) → platform context (admin operations, signup landing)
  • Tenant subdomain on platform (e.g., smithtravel.ai-travelconcierge.com) → that tenant
  • Tenant custom domain (e.g., travel.smithfamilyagency.com) → that tenant via DNS CNAME (per §16.3)

Tenant resolution happens in middleware and is cached for the request lifecycle. RLS policies enforce tenant scoping at the database level.

1.5 Multi-Tenancy Architecture ¶

The platform enforces tenant isolation through four layers (§26.2): identity (Supabase Auth), tenant context (middleware tenant resolution), authorization (assertPermission()), and data access scoping (tenantClient(ctx) plus RLS).

All tenant-scoped tables include a tenant_id column with RLS policies enforcing access. Two helper functions are used in policies: auth_user_in_tenant(tenant_id) checks the user has an active row in that tenant; tenant_is_active(tenant_id) checks the tenant itself is operational. See §5.1 for definitions and §5.1.2 for required policy coverage.

Database access from application code goes through typed clients per §5.4. Service-role usage discipline is defined in §26.3a.

The platform supports a strictly two-level structure: the platform, and direct tenants (BYO-host, sub-host, or the platform's own automated agency). No tenant nests beneath another tenant. If a sub-host engages subcontractors to help operate their agency, those subcontractors are users inside the sub-host's tenant — not separate tenants on the platform. The platform has no relationship with a sub-host's subcontractors.
